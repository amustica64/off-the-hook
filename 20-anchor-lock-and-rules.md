## The register, settled

Three positions were tried in three days: Michelin (Doc 18 v1), live-fire casual (Anne's grill reference), and back toward fine dining (Anne's nine-image reference set). The set that resolved it: her images 3 and 6, beetroot on dark reactive-glaze ceramic over dark wood, and potatoes on pea purée on speckled ceramic.

**The settled position: rustic food, composed plating.**

Fire-cooked, deeply spiced, generous ingredients. Plated with deliberate composition, intentional spacing, real negative space, and good warm rustic ceramic. Not stuffy, not tiny, not tweezered. Not a takeaway either.

The variable was never how posh the food is. It was how composed the plate is.

## Standing rule 1 — char is an accent, never a surface

The first generation came back reading as burnt. Cause was mine: the prompt stacked four blackening cues (blackened, char, dark, deep red-brown) and the model read that as an instruction to carbonise. A suya rub is already dark before it meets heat, so the model cannot separate spice crust from burn unless told.

**Rule.** In every fire frame, char is specified as a limited accent confined to edges and tips. The meat everywhere else is specified as juicy, blushing pink inside, surface glistening with rendered fat. The spice crust is specified separately as matte rust-red, terracotta and paprika, distinct from the black bits.

**Mandatory negatives on every fire frame:** no burnt meat, no carbonised surface, no blackened crust, no ash, no dry leathery meat, no overcooked grey interior.

## Standing rule 2 — no skewers anywhere in the set

Anne's rejection of the second frame was "it looks like kebab." Skewers read as takeaway regardless of how well they are lit or plated. Nothing else in the frame can outrun them.

**Rule.** Meat is sliced and fanned or arranged, never skewered. No metal skewers, no wooden skewers, no piled meat.

**Mandatory negatives on every meat frame:** no skewers, no metal skewer, no wooden skewer, no kebab, no pile of meat.

## Standing rule 3 — composed, not tweezered

Elements placed with intent and clean spacing. Sauce or purée spread deliberately as a swoosh or base, not dotted. Garnish is a few herb leaves and pickled elements placed with care.

**Mandatory negatives:** no edible flowers, no tweezer micro-garnish, no foam, no tapioca pearls, no sauce dots, no white porcelain, no white tablecloth.

The flower-and-tweezer plates in Anne's reference set are out of scope deliberately. They fight the honest-craft story the brand runs on, and they need a chef who can execute them every night of service.

## Surface and setting, locked

Wide dark reactive-glaze ceramic, or warm speckled cream ceramic. Dark aged wood beneath. Warm directional light from one side, roughly 4000K, raking so texture reads. Warm espresso shadows with detail held, cream highlights never pure white, a whisper of grain.
