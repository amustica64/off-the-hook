# Off the Hook — photography and image direction

Directed with the photography masters, taste-maker, the creative director and design-impeccable. This is the look, the rules, and the shot list. Read it before generating, shooting, or placing a single image. The one thing that will sink this brand faster than anything is slop imagery, so this document is strict on purpose.

## The look, in one line

Warm, editorial, documentary-real. A proper restaurant with a conscience, shot in honest window light, with the dignity of Sebastião Salgado brought to the people and the work, and the quiet editorial warmth of a good food magazine brought to the room and the plate. Not glossy, not staged, not stock. Every frame should look like it was taken by someone who was actually there.

## The reference bar

For the people and the work: Salgado. Natural light only, deep honest shadows, hands and labour treated with weight and respect. This is the register for anyone at the pass, any pair of hands, the training moments. Dignity is the whole point of this place, so the pictures carry it.

For the room and the food: the warm end of food editorial. Think the light and restraint of Kinfolk, Cereal and Toast, the honesty of St. John and Blacklock rather than a glossy commercial menu shoot. Real steam, real crumbs, a used napkin.

For the named portraits, Anne and the team: Leibovitz-lite. One considered light, a real expression, a real setting. Editorial, not a corporate headshot.

What we are not: not Gursky (too cold and corporate), not Crewdson (too staged and uncanny, it would read as fake), not the ink-and-drama of the reference reel, and never AI food-porn with a plastic sheen.

## Light logic, the thing that ties it all together

One believable source. Soft, directional daylight from a window, raking across the subject so form and texture read. Warm, around 3800 to 4600K, never the cold blue of an office or the orange-teal of a film LUT. Shadows are deep and warm, the colour of espresso, never muddy grey. If the light in two images disagrees, the set falls apart, so this is the non-negotiable through-line: soft, warm, side-on daylight, everywhere.

## Lens, angle, aspect, per subject

- Hero food and plates: 50mm, f2.8 to f4, slightly above the plate at 30 to 45 degrees, the honest angle you eat at. 4:5 for mobile heroes, 3:2 for wide. Steam and mess welcome.
- The room: 35mm, f4 to f5.6, eye level, from a corner so the tables lead the eye in. 3:2 or 16:9. Shot in the quiet before or after service, warm and lived-in.
- Hands at the pass, the work: 50mm, f2.8, close and eye level with the hands, Salgado weight. 4:5 or square. This is the emotional core of the site.
- Portraits (Anne, team, willing graduates): 85mm, f2, one soft key from the window, loop or Rembrandt shadow, real eye contact. 4:5.
- The training journey: a documentary sequence, 35mm, f4, eye level, honest and unstaged, following the actual steps of a real day.

## The grade

Warm and filmic, matched to the brand tokens. Cream, not white, in the highlights. Greens that sit happily beside the forest accent. Espresso-brown shadows with detail retained, never crushed to black. A whisper of grain for a printed, editorial feel. No heavy vignettes, no orange-teal, no Instagram filter. If a frame would look at home in a good cookbook, it is graded right.

## Composition, and how it serves the motion

Every hero and section image is composed with deliberate negative space on one side, cream-toned and quiet, because that is where the wordmark, the headline, and the drawing line will sit. The image holds the weight on one side, the type and the line breathe on the other. Compose loose, never fill the frame edge to edge, so the reveal has room to travel. One focal point per image, the same discipline as the motion.

## Shot list, by surface

- Home hero: the room in warm morning light, or a single hero plate, with clean negative space left or right for the line to draw across and open it.
- About: Anne's portrait, real and warm, plus one wide of the room.
- Restaurant and menu: three or four honest plates, one pass shot, one room detail (a laid table, a hand pouring).
- The academy and journey: the documentary sequence, referral to first shift to qualification to paid work, told in hands, faces where consented, and the work itself.
- Impact: one strong human frame behind the numbers, quiet enough for the count-up to read.
- Stories: a portrait or a pair of hands per story, consistent light so they sit as a set.
- Partners and hire: the room working, plates going out, the calibre visible without a word.

## The dignity and consent rule, non-negotiable

The trainees are people leaving prison. You do not show an identifiable face without informed, freely given consent, and even with it, you lead with dignity. Where consent is not given, tell the story through hands, the work, backs, the room, the plate. This is not a limitation, it is the register that makes the site trustworthy to a referral partner. And you never, ever generate a synthetic face to stand in for a real trainee. That is both slop and a lie, and it would break the safeguarding trust the whole project runs on.

## Anti-slop, anti-cliché, hard rules

No chef-hat clichés, no knife-and-fork icons, no prison bars or breaking chains, ever. No stock "diverse team pointing at a laptop". No glossy over-lit food-porn. No AI faces, no plastic skin, no six-fingered hands, no impossible reflections, no soft-plastic sheen. No cold light. If an image has the faint smell of a template or a generator, it does not ship.

## Sourcing, honestly

The right answer is a real shoot. Half a day with a good local photographer who understands natural light, in the actual restaurant, will produce the hero, the room, the pass, Anne's portrait, and a set of real plates. That single shoot is the highest-value spend on this whole project and it retires most of these placeholders in one go. I will write the photographer's brief from this document when you want it.

Until that shoot, higgsfield can hold the pages, but only within a strict lane: rooms, tables, textures, ingredients, a laid place setting, steam, linen, the hook and line as still-life. Details and environments, directed to the light logic above, labelled as placeholders. Never people who are meant to be real trainees, never anything that pretends to be documentary truth. Directed that tightly, generation is a stand-in for texture, not a substitute for the real, human heart of this site.

## Then it comes together

Real light, real people handled with dignity, real food, composed to leave room for the line to draw and the type to breathe. That is how the imagery, the motion, and the brand read as one posh, deliberate thing rather than a set of parts.
