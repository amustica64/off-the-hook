# Off the Hook — photographer shoot brief

A half-day shoot in the restaurant. This brief gives the photographer everything: the look, the light, the shot list, and the rules. It is drawn from Doc 15 (image direction). Hand it over as is.

## The brief in a sentence

Warm, editorial, documentary-real pictures of a working restaurant that trains people leaving prison. Honest window light, real food, real hands, dignity throughout. Think a good food magazine crossed with Sebastião Salgado's respect for the people who do the work. Not glossy, not staged, not stock.

## Light, the one rule

Natural window light only, soft and directional, raking across the subject. Warm, roughly 3800 to 4600K. Deep, warm shadows, never cold or grey. If daylight is thin, bounce it, do not replace it with hard flash. Every frame must share this light so the set hangs together.

## Grade for delivery

Warm and filmic. Cream in the highlights, not white. Espresso-brown shadows with detail kept. Greens that sit beside a forest green brand accent. A whisper of grain. No orange-teal, no heavy vignette, no filter.

## Kit

Full frame or medium format. Lenses: 35mm, 50mm, 85mm. Tripod for the room and low-light plates. Shoot raw. Aspect ratios: capture loose so we can crop to 4:5 (mobile heroes), 3:2 and 16:9 (wide).

## Shot list

1. The room, hero. 35mm, f4 to f5.6, eye level from a corner, tables leading in. Warm, in the quiet before or after service. Leave clean space on one side of frame for text. 3:2 and 16:9.
2. Hero plate. 50mm, f2.8 to f4, 30 to 45 degrees above, the angle you eat at. Steam, a little mess, real. 4:5 and 3:2.
3. Hands at the pass. 50mm, f2.8, close, eye level with the hands, plating or passing a dish. This is the emotional core, shoot plenty. Salgado weight. 4:5 and square.
4. Anne, portrait. 85mm, f2, one soft window key, loop or Rembrandt shadow, real eye contact, in the restaurant not against a backdrop. 4:5.
5. Team and willing graduates, portraits. Same recipe as Anne, consistent light so they read as a set. Consent first, always.
6. Three or four honest plates of the actual menu. 50mm, f2.8 to f4, same light. Real garnish, real plates.
7. Room details. 35mm and 50mm. A laid table, a hand pouring, linen, cutlery, a menu on the pass. The quiet texture shots that fill sections.
8. The work, documentary. 35mm, f4, follow a real prep-to-service sequence, unstaged. Hands, faces where consented, the graft itself.

## Composition note

Compose loose, one focal point per frame, deliberate empty space on one side. The website draws an animated line across these images and sets type in that space, so an image that is busy edge to edge cannot be used. Give us room.

## Dignity and consent, non-negotiable

Trainees are people leaving prison. No identifiable face without informed, freely given, written consent, and even then lead with dignity. Where consent is not given, tell the story in hands, backs, the work, the room. Brief everyone on set before you lift the camera.

## Do not

No chef-hat clichés, no knife-and-fork poses, no prison or chain imagery ever, no stock "team pointing at a laptop", no hard glossy food-porn lighting, no cold colour.

## Deliverables

Raw plus graded JPEGs, in 4:5, 3:2 and 16:9 crops, minimum 3000px on the long edge. Roughly: 6 room and detail, 6 plates, 10 to 15 hands and work, 4 to 6 portraits. Rights assigned to the CIC for web and print.
